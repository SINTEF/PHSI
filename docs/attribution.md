# Attribution and scientific adaptations

This package implements the methods used in the port-Hamiltonian comparison through a new public interface. It is an adaptation of the supplied methods and private comparison code, rather than a complete reproduction of any original research package.

- **Pseudo-Hamiltonian neural networks:** Eidnes, Stasik, Sterud, Bøhn and Riemer-Sørensen, [*Pseudo-Hamiltonian neural networks with state-dependent external forces*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2023.133673), 2023. The supplied [neural-network repository](https://github.com/SINTEF/pseudo-hamiltonian-neural-networks) informs structured neural energies and integration-scheme fitting. Both original MIT notices are preserved in `third_party/`. The comparison differentiates through every internal integration-scheme evaluation; the supplied neural-network implementation stops gradients through two inner evaluations. Energy and damping networks here use smooth hyperbolic-tangent hidden activations. The input waveform is measured separately; learning a prescribed scalar input gain differs from learning an arbitrary external-force network.
- **Pseudo-Hamiltonian system identification:** Holmsen, Eidnes and Riemer-Sørensen, [*Pseudo-Hamiltonian system identification*](https://doi.org/10.3934/jcd.2024001), 2024; [original source on the preserved legacy branch](https://github.com/SINTEF/PHSI/tree/legacy_phsi). The fixed polynomial Hamiltonian and structured identification draw on this work. The comparison fits the full library before applying independent component thresholds and joint refits with fixed masks. It does not use the original repeated-pruning training schedule. The damping hybrid places the neural network in damping, whereas the original paper demonstrated a neural external-force term.
- **Sparse identification of port-Hamiltonian systems:** Myhr, Eidnes and Kutz, [*SIPHy: Sparse identification of port-Hamiltonian systems from noisy data*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20399), 2026 supplied preprint. The joint canonical regression substitutes observed position rates for momentum energy gradients in damping columns. Hamiltonian flow splines from the supplied method are omitted. Finite-difference or declared Savitzky–Golay derivative estimates, midpoint samples, independent component threshold grids, nonnegative physical damping bounds and empty supports are disclosed adaptations. No public source-repository availability is asserted for the supplied code.
- **Neural Hamiltonian ordinary differential equations:** Meltzer, Eidnes and Stasik, [*Learning partially observed systems with neural Hamiltonian ordinary differential equations*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23510), 2026 supplied preprint. Trajectory fitting informed the comparison, but this implementation uses fully observed states, measured inputs and damping. Its simulation-error objective differentiates directly through discrete explicit fourth-order Runge–Kutta steps; it is not a continuous-adjoint solver or a reproduction of the supplied JAX scripts.

The midpoint regression objective uses observed increment rates in damping columns so the joint problem remains linear. It is therefore different from fitting the original nonlinear port-Hamiltonian midpoint residual. Regression requires the known canonical position/momentum split and diagonal damping; its derivation is not claimed for arbitrary interconnection matrices or partial state observations.

All analytic energy, damping and input-gain libraries are pruned when present, with independent normalized-contribution thresholds. Constant damping scalars and neural weights are excluded. Empty supports remain valid. Every candidate is selected from its complete validation grid, with fixed deterministic tie rules, timed checkpoint eligibility and durable saves. The unrefitted full model is separately eligible for Adam fitting. This package keeps the numerical policies, while excluding campaign queues, benchmark truth, historical payload paths, private observation exports and held-out evaluation from its runtime.

The circuit's constitutive motivation follows Castaños, Jayawardhana, Ortega and García-Canseco, [*Proportional plus integral control for set-point regulation of a class of nonlinear RLC circuits*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00034-009-9103-x), 2009, equations (2.17)–(2.18). Here RLC means resistor–inductor–capacitor. The comparison adapts its hyperbolic-tangent current–flux law and log-cosh energy to a simpler two-state series circuit. The obsolete thesis equation citation is not used. Benchmark truth and generation remain outside the general fitting runtime.

The public package additionally accepts arbitrary even state dimensions and explicit measured-input interpolation, and stores complete resolved function definitions, scales, masks and initialization identities for reload. Scientific equivalence tests establish bounded component and numerical contracts; they do not establish convergence, forecast rankings, scientific acceptance or reproduction of paper tables. Alternating hybrid fitting and separate constrained-energy studies are not included in the supported comparison. Nonnegative damping alone is not advertised as a guarantee of bounded states or a globally lower-bounded analytic Hamiltonian.

Source identities and the adaptation map are in `docs/source-provenance.json`. The project owner’s reuse authorization is recorded in `docs/license-status.md`; existing upstream notices remain preserved.
